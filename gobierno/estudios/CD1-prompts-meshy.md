## 1. Templo toscano (Roma, siglo III a.C.)
```
Roman Republican Tuscan temple, 3rd century BC, game asset seen from above. High stone podium of grey-brown tufa with a
single wide staircase only at the front. Deep front porch with two rows of four thick, widely spaced Tuscan columns,
plain capitals, no fluting, painted stucco. Behind the porch, three side-by-side cellae under one roof. Wide, low
triangular pediment with painted terracotta statues on the roof ridge and corners. Overhanging timber roof covered with
red-orange terracotta tiles, with decorated terracotta antefixes along the eaves. No marble, no white Greek look, no
Corinthian columns, no arches. Warm afternoon light, clean simple shapes, mobile game, low poly.
```

## 2. Curia (Curia Hostilia, sede del Senado, siglo III a.C.)
```
Roman Republican senate house (Curia Hostilia), 3rd century BC, game asset seen from above. Tall rectangular hall of
grey-brown tufa blocks with white lime stucco on the front. Steep gable roof of red terracotta tiles. A short flight of
stone steps leading to a large double bronze door in the front wall, three high narrow windows above the door. Plain
walls, simple stone cornice, small raised platform in front for speakers. No marble, no columns on the front, no domes,
no arches. Warm afternoon light, clean simple shapes, mobile game, low poly.
```

## Dictamen histórico (provisional; lo coteja la skill `historia` en CD1)
- Templo: podio alto, escalera solo delante, columnas toscanas muy separadas, tres celdas, terracota pintada en el
  tejado (Vitrubio 4.7; templo de Júpiter Capitolino). Mármol solo desde 146 a.C., como ya fija CI1.
- Curia Hostilia: sala rectangular junto al Comicio, tradición atribuida a Tulo Hostilio (Livio 1.30; Varrón, *Lengua
  latina* 5.155). Su aspecto exacto no se conoce: forma de la Curia Julia, más tardía, simplificada y sin mármol.
